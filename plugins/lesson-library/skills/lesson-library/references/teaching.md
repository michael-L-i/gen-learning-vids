# Teaching a lesson at the requested level

## Calibrate knowledge separately from pace

Use the current request and conversation first, then relevant profile evidence.
Distinguish declared knowledge, demonstrated understanding, provisional
prerequisites suggested by the task, and unresolved gaps. A missing profile is
not evidence of beginner status; notes and watched videos are not evidence of
mastery. An advanced question licenses provisional use of its normal vocabulary,
not an assumption that the learner already understands its solution.

Choose what to explain from the learner's question. Skip definitions of established
prerequisites; spend time on unfamiliar decisions, connections and failure points.
An advanced learner may want familiar terminology with slow, explicit reasoning.
For an introductory question, build the relevant model before manipulating symbols.
Do not turn one lesson's feedback into a permanent, global ability rating.

## Plan the explanation before the pictures

Record a compact teaching plan: learner evidence and assumptions, the specific
focus, prerequisites to skip, an approach justified by the dependencies between
ideas, and where processing time is needed. Keep this authoring metadata out of
ordinary narration. Choose a structure suited to the task; there is no mandatory
introductory refresher, chapter count, word budget, or subject-specific sequence.
Honor a requested duration by narrowing scope when necessary, not compressing all
the reasoning. Technical schema limits are ceilings, not targets.

Work backward from what the learner should be able to explain or do. Identify the
hard transitions. For a meaningful change, make its reason and resulting state
clear. State why a method or representation is useful before relying on it. Show
enough intermediate work to bridge this learner's gap; omit routine detail they
already know. Avoid narrating algebra while silently changing the underlying model.

## Keep the learner oriented

Make the question clear before solving it: what is given, what is happening, and
what the learner is trying to find. Show essential conditions on the relevant
objects. Familiar subject vocabulary does not replace a clear problem statement.

Let the content provide orientation. Preserve the relevant diagram, givens,
previous result or comparison state through a transition and highlight the change.
An opening agenda can help a long explanation, but is optional. Do not default to
numbered footers, frame counters, or a persistent route through every stage. Use
brief subgoal cues or recaps only where they help reconnect the reasoning.

Introduce symbols at first use with their meaning and units when applicable.
Keep names, colors, coordinate conventions and object identities consistent.
Label the actual object or arrow referred to by speech. Familiarity with a formula
does not tell the learner what its variables represent in this particular problem.
Retain a compact legend where referring back is necessary. Reveal an equation,
structure or diagram feature when its narration begins, and keep it available
while explaining it. Avoid showing the answer before a prediction opportunity.

When motion or change is central to the question, animate that behavior to make
the reasoning visible. Give the learner time to watch; replay, pause, trace paths,
or compare states when useful. Moving labels should stay attached to their objects.
Connect the visible change to the quantities being discussed, rather than adding
decorative motion around equations. Keep physical time, trajectories and constraints
consistent; identify slow motion or schematic views when they could mislead.

## Pace changes, not just speech

Author beats around meaningful explanatory changes. Give each beat a clear visual
focus and a reason to move on; do not split solely by word count. Several sentences
may belong to one beat. Use a processing hold after a new representation, a dense
inference, a comparison, or a question. Keep routine transitions brisk. Do not add
the same long silence everywhere or globally slow the voice to hide a dense script.

Use measured narration boundaries for reveals. `pauseAfter` is processing time
after speech, not an estimate of speech duration. Word positions inside a beat
remain approximate: split at a needed reveal instead of timing it to a guessed
word timestamp. Author deterministic visual states and inspect the boundaries,
especially whether needed labels survive and whether the next answer appears early.

## Revise before rendering

Read the draft as this learner, following both speech and planned visual states.
Remove unnecessary prerequisite explanations. Repair any transition that relies
on an unstated inference, undefined symbol, vanished result or ambiguous pointer.
Check that the viewer can state the problem and follow what changes on screen;
a labeled still diagram alone may not explain the motion under discussion.
Give the hardest reasoning appropriate time, and check that the final question
tests the objective without revealing its answer prematurely. Revise actual
content and timing; a positive review label is not evidence of quality.

Use a bounded loop: draft, focused revision, then inspect rendered evidence. Repeat
only to repair a concrete issue found in the script, frames, audio or timing.
State unresolved assumptions honestly. Structural validation cannot certify
pedagogical or scientific correctness. Verify the underlying subject separately.

## Calibration examples (illustrations, not templates)

- Introductory geometry: connect the labeled sides to the relationship before
  substituting numbers, and explain why taking a square root recovers a length.
- Advanced synthesis: assume familiar functional-group vocabulary when supported
  by the request; explain strategy, selectivity and the consequence of each change.
- A process explanation: make the relevant stages and what passes between them
  visible, rather than rapidly listing all components.
- A physics derivation: establish the frame and symbols, then spend time on the
  modeling choice or approximation that makes the calculation possible.
